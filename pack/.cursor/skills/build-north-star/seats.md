# Seats

Staffing chooses which seats sit. These playbooks are **optional charters**,
not a mandatory three. Invent a seat with eight lines if none fits
(name, job, objection duty, do-not). Max five present.

Every present seat applies the six kernel lenses. Write what was learned.
Do not pad rows to a taxonomy.

Prefix every question with the seat name. One at a time. One real objection
before you leave the seat.

---

## Product (playbook)

Job: make the vision small enough to be true.

Lenses to stress: **purpose**, then judgment (who decides v1).

Ask (adapt): who is in pain (operational sentence)? What does “done” look
like in the world? What will we be tempted to add (non-goals)? User of v1
vs audience of the pitch? One promise we keep if we can keep only one?

**Objection:** a product that satisfies the pitch and is still the wrong business.

Do not: pick a stack, list screens, expand into a platform.

---

## Engineering (playbook)

Job: strategic design. Deep modules, hide complexity, delete illegal states.

Lenses to stress: **complexity**, **belief**.

Ask (adapt): smallest contract v1 exposes? What must stay inside? What
illegal states can we delete? What must unit N never know about N+1?
Where will an agent invent a second path?

**Objection:** a ladder sliced by layer or tool. Demand slice by contract,
ordered by **risk retired**.

Do not: file trees, frameworks, `/unit-unlock`.

---

## Platform (playbook)

Job: name substrates — anything that remembers, bills, retries, schedules,
or whose failure the domain will misread. May be a database, a vector index,
a model API, a corpus, a queue, or nothing durable (write that default).

Lenses to stress: **unknowns**, **operate** (only for named substrates).

Ask (adapt): what remembers? what is the same work twice? what goes stale?
what brain do we not own? what runs when nobody is watching? what must not
enter logs, prompts, or indexes? Gift vocabulary; open official docs.

**Objection:** how this vision dies if a substrate stays unnamed.

Do not: invent a schema to look concrete. Do not assume Postgres.
